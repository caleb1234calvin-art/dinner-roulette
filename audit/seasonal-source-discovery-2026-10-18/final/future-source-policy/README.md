## Three candidate access routes

### 1. Existing Private.coffee Overpass — useful for metadata/proposals

[Published terms and privacy policy](https://overpass.private.coffee/) explicitly permit commercial projects without registration or a key. The documented endpoint is `https://overpass.private.coffee/api/interpreter`. Terms dated 10 April 2026 require optimized queries, limited concurrency, no PII and OSM licensing compliance. They prefer POST and a project-identifying User-Agent with contact information; require users to receive or be directed to the provider privacy policy; and request advance notice for large operations, exemplified by over 10 requests/second or 10 concurrent requests. No numerical general quota is imposed. This is best-effort service, with potentially stale data and no warranty.

The complete policy page was directly retrieved: HTTP 200, 13,402 bytes, SHA-256 `0be79668423e1bc2fdb56a30114759c2f3540024f325b446dca79fe11e470e90`. A bounded direct robots request returned 404. That is no retrieved robots rule, not proof of unlimited permission; the affirmative API terms are the access basis. Details are in the two direct-read records.

**Scope:** mapped place identifiers, names, coordinates, classifications, lifecycle tags and recorded URLs can support provenance-bearing proposals. An OSM website tag is only a reference, never a crawler grant. This is already an underlying source in the app; another adapter alone adds no demonstrated inventory or season coverage.

**Reuse:** [OSM copyright guidance](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) permits copying/adaptation with contributor attribution and ODbL notice. The [ODbL](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/) permits retention and commercial reuse subject to its conditions. Public use of a derivative database can require share-alike plus machine-readable database/alteration access; collective databases and produced works have distinctions. Do not assume merely keeping separate files resolves those obligations. Preserve notices and source provenance, and review the actual combination/publication design before release. No rights to third-party images or descriptions are established.

### 2. Geofabrik public OSM extracts — later local-processing option

[The download service](https://download.geofabrik.de/) explicitly supplies free ODbL OSM extracts, usually refreshed daily. Its [technical documentation](https://download.geofabrik.de/technical.html) describes public no-login files, regional PBFs and incremental updates. Contributor user, uid and changeset fields are excluded from public files; the separate authenticated metadata/history service is outside scope.

No API key or operator contact is needed for documented public downloads. No numerical request quota was found in the two inspected pages; that is not an unlimited-use finding. A later pilot would select one smallest relevant regional file, verify that exact path and size, and retain/reuse it under the same ODbL obligations. No file, index, diff or region inventory was downloaded. This route changes transport/caching, not source independence or event authority; it is less minimal than the existing mirror.

### 3. NPS official events API — genuine event schema, credentials pending

[NPS describes external reuse of its API](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/digital/nps-data-api.htm). The [official Swagger document](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/customcf/swagger.json?03142019) defines GET `https://developer.nps.gov/api/v1/events`, location/date/category filters, `pageSize`/`pageNumber`, and recurrence expansion. Its event schema includes IDs, explicit dates, recurrence, times, nullable coordinates and update metadata. These are schema capabilities, not verified 2026 records.

The [API guide](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/guides.htm) requires a private key, documents header authentication and a default 1,000 requests/hour across that key, usage headers, rolling limits and HTTP 429. No existing key was sought or assumed; no registration, key creation, configuration or API request was performed. Those access steps need separate authorization/secure handling. Offline contract tests can be designed without them.

The guide's terms link to the [NPS disclaimer](https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/disclaimer.htm): NPS-created government works are generally public domain unless indicated otherwise, but third-party rights and protected marks remain. Acknowledgement is appreciated; commercial republication has an original-government-work copyright-notice requirement. A future adapter should initially retain only necessary factual fields and provenance, excluding media, lengthy descriptions and contacts. NPS coverage concerns NPS-associated events, not a comprehensive private-haunt/farm inventory. The [FAQ](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/faqs.htm) describes possible two-hour API lag, but is dated 2017; it is not a current freshness SLA. Live dates, cancellations, timezone and category eligibility would still require validation.

## Existing-provider comparison and limitations

- The [German Overpass instance's guidance](https://dev.overpass-api.de/overpass-doc/en/preface/commons.html) gives broad safety margins around 10,000 requests/day and 1 GB/day, dynamic slot/cooldown limits and HTTP 429. It warns against relying on its public instances as a consumer-app backend. Do not generalize that operator-specific warning to Private.coffee, whose own terms differ. The French and Mail.ru hosts were not reviewed or queried.
- [Public Nominatim policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/) permits moderate user-triggered geocoding, at most one request/second for the entire application, with identifying headers, attribution and switchable service. Cache; no autocomplete, systematic POI harvesting or personal/confidential input. Small bulk jobs have additional single-machine/thread limits; periodic/long-running jobs are restricted to four requests/minute. Its current LLM section requires an informed developer decision and disallows generic no-code/vibe-platform geocoding offerings. This is location lookup, not a new seasonal source. Historical success tests do not establish present policy compliance.
- At the frozen head, `src/lib/date-night/search.ts` and `src/lib/discovery/hedged-provider.ts` already use Private.coffee as the second of four mirrors. A Date Night RPC may run four groups, each with one attempt per mirror: up to four requests to this host per RPC, not a global application cap. Hedging occurs at 1.5-second offsets; attempts have eight-second limits within a 20-second budget. Dinner/nightlife also use it. Aggregate traffic across users/processes remains unmeasured.
- Inspected provider files establish POST and identifying product names, but their User-Agents lack a contact URL. No cross-user host limiter, `Retry-After` handling or server cache was established in those paths. This limited inspection does not establish aggregate traffic or a policy violation. Attribution remains a separate rendered-integration check; no live page was tested.
- The separate [prepared PR82 policy source](https://github.com/caleb1234calvin-art/dinner-roulette/blob/cf18ff1c5a56ecf59f8006d4f7c83fb289f393df/src/lib/legal/documents.json), independently read at exact head `cf18ff1c5a56ecf59f8006d4f7c83fb289f393df`, already names every configured mirror, describes coordinate/radius/category transfers and distinguishes operators. **Do not claim a missing provider disclosure.** Its location section links OSMF privacy; no direct Private.coffee policy URL was found in that document. Whether the existing wording/reference satisfies the provider's specific policy-reference instruction is a narrow future integration review item, not a declared critical blocker. PR82 remains separate, draft and unmerged; prepared publication metadata is not live publication. No PR82 change is proposed or made here.
- Existing queries can use caller-derived coordinates. Coordinates alone do not establish a PII-policy violation; identifiability depends on context, and no actual user traffic was inspected. A prospective research adapter can avoid that question by using fixed public geographic areas, without owner snapshots, saved location, identities or user-derived coordinates.

No page following and no inference that OSM update time proves current-season operation.
